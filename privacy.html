Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026. This policy covers the Auditly app for Android (package uk.jj.auditly) and this website. Auditly is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Auditly does not hold Android's internet permission. It cannot go online, so it cannot send anything anywhere.
  • Everything it works out about your apps stays on your phone. Its records are encrypted.
  • There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics.

What stays on your phone

Auditly keeps its timeline of changes, the last snapshot of your apps, tracker results and your settings in the app's private storage. The timeline, snapshot and tracker results are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held in Android's Keystore. Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for Auditly.

Reports

PDF, spreadsheet and JSON reports are made only when you ask, and saved where you choose. They list your apps and their permissions. Once saved, they are yours to keep, share or delete.

Links to this website

Settings, About has Check for updates, Website and More apps. Each opens this site in your browser; Auditly itself never goes online.

Keeping and deleting your data

Clear the timeline in Settings, or use Erase everything to remove all of Auditly's records. Uninstalling Auditly deletes everything it holds.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no scripts beyond the app list. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, for security, under GitHub's privacy statement.
